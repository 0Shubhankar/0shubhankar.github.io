The simulation renders a hypothetical urban grid where users can adjust variables to simulate changes in urban environments. The primary interactive elements include:

Geometric Configuration: Users can modify the grid's layout by adjusting the number of rows and columns, altering the urban area's scope.

Economic Indicators: Sliders allow manipulation of median home prices and rent prices, reflecting the neighborhood's economic status.

Infrastructure Quality: Adjustments to walk scores and transit scores depict the accessibility and quality of local transportation.

Demographic Characteristics: Variables such as education levels and marital status influence the demographic profile of the population within the simulation.

These parameters are represented visually within the simulation; buildings, modeled as cubes on the grid, change in height and color based on the values set by the user. The height variation represents urban density, while color changes indicate economic and demographic shifts.

Changes in the simulation are instantaneous, providing immediate visual feedback in response to user adjustments. This dynamic response is key to understanding the impact of different variables on urban gentrification:

Building Color Changes: Represent areas undergoing socio-economic changes. A shift towards brighter or more saturated colors typically signifies neighborhoods experiencing gentrification, characterized by an influx of wealthier residents and rising property values.

Building Height Adjustments: Reflect the development intensity of different parts of the urban grid, where taller buildings might represent denser, possibly more developed or affluent areas.

Gentrification within the simulation is calculated using a composite "gentrification rate" derived from the economic, infrastructure, and demographic indicators:

Economic Contributions: Higher property and rental costs directly increase the gentrification likelihood, reflecting the real-world scenario where rising costs can displace original residents.

Infrastructure Contributions: Enhanced walkability and better transit connectivity make neighborhoods more desirable, potentially accelerating gentrification.

Demographic Contributions: The simulation presupposes that higher education levels and certain marital statuses correlate with higher incomes, which can contribute to gentrification pressures.

Housing Type Influence: Certain types of housing, such as condominiums, are modeled to be more susceptible to quick value increases, which can be indicative of gentrification.

By enabling real-time interaction with these urban parameters, the simulation offers a visual method to explore how different factors influence urban gentrification. Users can see how changes in policy or economic conditions might visually and structurally impact a neighborhood, simulating the complex interplay of forces that drive urban evolution.
