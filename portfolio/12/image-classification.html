Getting Started

Deep learning models are great at interpreting and classifying different environments, and they’re especially interesting for analyzing the landscapes of cities. This project takes the pre-trained Places365 ResNet50 model (Zhou et al., 2017) for a spin to see how well it can classify images from Indian cities—using pictures straight from Wikipedia! The goal? To find out how well a globally trained model handles the unique and complex vibes of Indian urban areas.

What Makes Indian Cities Special?

Indian cities are a mix of culture, architecture, and planning. Each one feels unique, from the streets to the markets and temples. While the ResNet50 model, trained on the Places365 dataset, is great at spotting general urban features, it sometimes struggles with city-specific details—especially in places like India where the landscapes are diverse and full of character. This project checks how well the model handles those specifics.

How I Did It

I used the Places365 pre-trained ResNet50 model to classify images of Indian cities. Here’s how it went down:

1. Loading the Model: I used the ResNet50 model with its 365 place categories, ready to analyze images based on its training across a range of scenes.

2. Getting the Images Ready: I pulled images from Wikipedia entries of different Indian cities and prepped them to meet the model's requirements.

3. Letting the Model Do Its Thing: I fed the images to the model and got back the top 10 possible categories, paying close attention to how well it recognized places like markets, temples, and bustling urban streets.

What Happened?

The model did pretty well in identifying common city elements like buildings, streets, and vehicles. But when it came to pictures with cultural or historical vibes—well, it wasn’t so sure. A lot of those images don’t exactly scream "urban," so the model struggled a bit, leading to some misclassifications.

What I Learned

This experiment showed that global models like ResNet50 can be a little too broad when it comes to specific places like Indian cities. The distinct look and feel of Indian architecture and city life don’t always match up with what the model was originally trained on. To fix this, we probably need to:

- Train It Locally: Integrating locally relevant images into the training dataset could significantly enhance the model's capacity to accurately classify region-specific features (Zhou et al., 2017).

- Fine-Tune the Model: Adjust the model to focus on the specific urban features of Indian cities to make its predictions more accurate and useful.

Final Thoughts

This was a fun project that highlighted both the power and the limitations of using global pre-trained models for local tasks. With a bit of fine-tuning and more specific training data, models like ResNet50 can become much better at recognizing the unique features of Indian cityscapes.

My Takeaway

Next time, I plan to add even more images that focus on urban themes. This project started from a curiosity about how well these models perform in city contexts, and while they do okay, there’s definitely room for improvement! The model shows promise, but it needs a bit of tweaking to truly understand the nuances of Indian city life.
