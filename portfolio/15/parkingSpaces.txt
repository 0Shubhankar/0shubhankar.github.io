Analyzing urban parking through various ratios can provide deep insights into city planning, transportation efficiency, and urban mobility. Here are a few additional ratios and metrics that could be useful alongside the parking-to-car and population-to-car ratios:

### Additional Ratios and Metrics:

1. **Parking Space per Capita**: This ratio measures the amount of parking space available per resident and can help in assessing whether there is a surplus or shortage of parking in relation to the population size.

2. **Car-to-Household Ratio**: This provides information on how many cars are owned per household, which can vary widely in urban versus suburban settings.

3. **Utilization Rate of Parking**: Measures how much of the available parking space is actually used, which can indicate the efficiency of parking space allocation.

4. **Turnover Rate of Parking Spots**: This ratio can help determine how frequently parking spots are vacated and filled again, which is critical for understanding parking dynamics in busy areas.

5. **Cost of Parking per Square Meter**: Analyzing the cost associated with parking spaces can give insights into the economic impact of parking in city planning.

6. **Public Transport Accessibility Index**: This can be calculated by determining the availability and frequency of public transport within walking distance from parking spots. This index can help understand how well integrated parking areas are with other modes of transportation.

7. **Vehicle Miles Traveled per Parking Space**: This would measure how much vehicle movement occurs per parking space available, providing insights into the usage intensity of roads relative to parking capacity.

8. **Ratio of Parking Areas to Total Built-up Area**: This gives a sense of how much of the city's land is dedicated to parking compared to other uses, which is crucial for sustainable urban planning.

- **Transportation and Urban Planning Departments**: Local government databases often have detailed records of vehicle registrations, household surveys, and parking infrastructures.
- **Census Data**: Provides detailed demographic and household data, including vehicle ownership.
- **Economic Surveys**: Data on property values and costs associated with land use, including parking.
- **Public Transport Records**: Information on public transport routes, schedules, and usage statistics.
- **Traffic Management Systems**: Data on road usage, traffic flows, and parking spot turnovers.

Using these ratios and gathering data from reliable sources will allow for a comprehensive analysis of parking and its implications on urban environments. This approach can aid in making informed decisions regarding urban development, transportation policies, and infrastructure investments.
