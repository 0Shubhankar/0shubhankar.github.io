The Krauss Car-Following Model provides a framework for simulating the interactions between vehicles in traffic based on their speeds and the distances between them. This model's primary goal is to calculate a safe operating speed for each vehicle that prevents collisions while allowing traffic to flow as freely as possible.

Safe Speed Calculation: This calculation is central to preventing rear-end collisions by determining the maximum safe speed a vehicle can travel given the current traffic conditions: $$v_{safe} = \frac{s_i(t) \cdot v_i(t)}{s_i(t) + \tau \cdot v_i(t)}$$

Here:

  • s_i(t) is the distance to the vehicle in front, ensuring that there is enough space to stop or slow down if the leading vehicle decelerates.
  • v_i(t) is the current speed of the vehicle, integrating the vehicle's ability to react within a safe margin.
  • tau represents the reaction time, allowing for driver response before adjustments in speed are required.

Acceleration/Deceleration Dynamics: The model also determines the appropriate acceleration or deceleration needed at each timestep to adjust the vehicle's speed towards the calculated v_{safe}: $$a_i(t) = \min \left( a_{max}, \frac{v_{safe} - v_i(t)}{\Delta t} \right)$$

  • a_{max} is the maximum acceleration/deceleration the vehicle can safely achieve.
  • Delta t is the timestep in seconds, providing the interval over which speed adjustments are calculated.

Visualization of Waiting Times: The simulation uses color coding to represent the waiting time of vehicles, reflecting periods when vehicles are stationary or moving much slower than the calculated v_{safe}: $$T_{wait} = \sum_{\text{all } t \text{ where } v_i(t) = 0} \Delta t$$ The colors transition as follows:

  • Blue (0-30 seconds): Indicates minimal waiting times, typical of moving traffic.
  • Cyan (30-100 seconds): Represents short-term stopping, possibly at traffic signals.
  • Shades of Green (100-200 seconds): Signify increasing waiting times, likely in stop-and-go traffic.
  • Yellow (200-300 seconds): Points to longer delays, common in heavier traffic.
  • Red (>300 seconds): Denotes extensive waiting times, typical of traffic jams or congested intersections.

This method of visualization provides immediate, understandable feedback on the impact of traffic conditions over the course of the simulation, allowing observers to quickly gauge the effectiveness of current traffic management strategies and the general flow of traffic in a complex urban environment like Connaught Place, Delhi.
