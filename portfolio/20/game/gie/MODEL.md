# Greenville environmental extension
All parameters are illustrative and editable in ecology.js. The fictional city has no observed calibration data. The network is a district-level 11x11 orthogonal street grid, not the UUE rendered roads; each node has equal population weight. Park entrances are allocated deterministically as park hectares increase, with one entrance per 8 ha and at most 13 entrances. Manhattan shortest path is exact on this assumed grid; the 300 m threshold is literature-informed (Konijnendijk 2023, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11676-022-01523-z). No actual neighbourhood accessibility claim is made. Existing quality-adjusted access proxy and score weights retained for comparability.
Cooling = 0.045 C per canopy percentage point + 0.025 C per park-area percentage point times condition. This is an illustrative cooling potential relative to zero vegetation, not a weather or temperature prediction. Carbon = canopy hectares times 2 tonnes C/ha/year; annual gross estimate, not net CO2e or cumulative storage. No tree inventory, age, species or mortality model. Runoff = rainfall depth (30 mm) times area times land-cover coefficients (developed .75, free .25, park .20). Coefficients are scenarios, not local hydrological calibration; canopy is not added separately to avoid double-counting. Habitat = bounded nonlinear park-area index weighted by condition and canopy, not species richness. Stakeholder scores are outcome proxies, not survey responses. Benchmark is identical elapsed years without intervention. No new policy choices or changes to the original scoring function.
Calibration requires observed pedestrian network, entrance and population data; measured canopy and temperature; tree inventories and growth; local rainfall, soil and runoff; species/habitat observations; and stakeholder data. Estimate parameters on training observations and verify against held-out observations. Sensitivity scenarios should vary each parameter; no empirical validation claimed.
