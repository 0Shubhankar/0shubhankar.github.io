Reading the model

How do jobs shape housing choices?

Households balance housing cost against access to employment. Moving a workplace changes the cost of reaching it, even when residential districts and transport routes remain fixed.

Travel time

Tᵢ = min(Tᵢ⁽ʳ⁾, Tᵢ⁽ᵗ⁾)
Tᵢ⁽ʳ⁾ = 4 + Dᵢ(2.55 + 2.55c)

Tᵢ is the shortest commute from residential district i. Road travel uses street-grid distance Dᵢ and congestion c (scaled from 0 to 1). Transit time compares every pair of stations on the fixed corridor, including access and onward travel. The faster option determines the displayed time.

Access and rent

Aᵢ = max(0, 1 − Tᵢ / 65)
Rᵢ = Rᵢ⁽⁰⁾ (1 + (50 − h)/260)(1 + 0.56εAᵢ)

Aᵢ measures proximity to the workplace, Rᵢ⁽⁰⁾ is the fixed baseline rent, h is housing supply (0–100), and ε is proximity sensitivity (0–1). More access raises modeled rent; more supply reduces it under these illustrative assumptions.

Affordability

φᵢ = Rᵢ / Y,   affordable if φᵢ ≤ 0.30

Y is the household income index. The 30% rule is a simplified screening threshold, not a complete measure of housing burden. It does not include out-of-pocket commuting costs.

The city, network distances, station locations, speeds, rents and coefficients are synthetic. This is a spatial thought experiment, not observed GIS data or a housing price forecast.
