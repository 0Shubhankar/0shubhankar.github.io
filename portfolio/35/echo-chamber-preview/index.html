How echo chambers emerge

Each dot is a person with an opinion, income, profession, location, and other characteristics. Connections are more likely between similar people. The resulting network can form tightly connected communities whose members encounter fewer differing views.

1. Connection probability

For people i and j, similarity is a weighted average of five factors:

Sij = ∑k=15 wksk,ij / ∑k=15 wk

wk is a preference weight and sk,ij is similarity on that characteristic (from 0 to 1). The model also accounts for differences in opinion and openness:

δij = |oi − oj| / 2
rij = (ui + uj) / 2 × U
qij = clip[Sij(1 − 0.48δij) + 0.34rijδij, 0, 1]
Pij = clip[D(qij)2.4 × 0.45, 0, 0.45]

o is opinion (−1 to +1), u is individual openness, U is the openness slider divided by 100, and D is connection density divided by 100. A reproducible draw decides whether a possible tie forms; each person can have at most eight ties.

2. Detecting echo chambers

The model detects groups from actual network connections. A group qualifies as an echo chamber when it contains at least four people, its internal connections dominate, and its opinions are relatively similar.

ηg = Ein,g / (Ein,g + Eout,g) ≥ 0.70
μg = (1/ng) ∑i∈g oi
MADg = (1/ng) ∑i∈g |oi − μg| < 0.46

Ein,g counts links inside group g; Eout,g counts links to outsiders. μg is the mean opinion and MADg is the mean absolute deviation. The code also requires at least two internal links. Shaded boundaries mark the qualifying chambers.

3. Measuring polarization

Polarization here measures opinion separation across the full population—not the number of red lines. With opinions ranging from −1 to +1:

Π = 100 × [2 / n(n − 1)] ∑i<j |oi − oj| / 2

The cross-view statistic instead counts actual connections between opposite sides of the opinion spectrum:

χ = 100 × Eopposite / Eall

Π measures how different people's opinions are; χ measures how often people on opposite sides connect. A divided population can still have bridge connections.

4. Explore the network

Increase political preference to favor similar opinions, or increase openness to encourage connections across differences. Switch between Chambers, Polarization, and Bridges to see the same network from different perspectives; the view buttons do not change its connections.

This is an illustrative model, not a prediction about a real society. The coefficients and classification thresholds are assumptions, and opinions do not update over time. The experiment examines network sorting and connection patterns rather than changing beliefs.
