Affordability & Migration

Where might households choose to move as housing prices, incomes, vacancy and neighbourhood density change?

This experiment explores that question across six hypothetical districts. It uses a simple location-choice model to show how changing economic conditions can shift the relative appeal of different places.

Exploring the city

Adjust the four controls and compare how the routes and district measures respond. A value of 1.0 represents the starting scenario. Returning all controls to 1.0 restores it. The 3D positions are schematic and do not represent geographical distances.

Property prices and annual income affect the relative cost of housing; vacancy indicates available housing; and population density provides a simplified measure of remaining capacity. These are deliberately limited representations of a much more complex housing market.

The model

For each origin district i, the model assigns a utility to every other district j. The first term penalizes expensive housing relative to origin income. Vacancy and capacity increase utility, while large differences in district income reduce it.

Uij = −α(Pj/Yi) + β(vj/0.20) + γhj − δ|ln(Yj/Yi)|

Pj is the property price at the destination; Yi and Yj are origin and destination annual incomes; vj is the destination vacancy fraction; and hj = max(0, 1 − ρj/ρj,max) is normalized density headroom.

Destination utilities are converted into conditional probabilities using a multinomial logit model:

pij = exp(Uij) / ∑k≠i exp(Uik)

The five alternative destinations sum to 100% for each origin. This is the probability of choosing a destination given that a move occurs; the model does not estimate whether someone moves in the first place.

The illustrative coefficient values are α = 0.45, β = 0.60, γ = 0.80 and δ = 0.30. They are not estimated from observed household decisions.

Interpreting the results

The moving spheres illustrate the distribution of destination choices; they do not measure migration volumes or travel time. Green, amber and red indicate broad categories of destination price-to-income ratios, not overall neighbourhood quality.

This is a stylized experiment, not a forecast. It excludes the option to stay, rents, household differences, credit conditions and commuting costs. The model also makes the usual multinomial logit independence assumption. Testing real migration patterns would require observed origin–destination data and estimated parameters.
