Overview of the Simulation

This simulation models an insurgency scenario where different groups of individuals (represented by stick figures) interact and transition between categories based on socio-political factors. The population consists of four main groups:

  • Supporters (Green): People who support the current regime.
  • Dissidents (Orange): People who oppose the regime but are not yet involved in violence.
  • Insurgents (Red): People who are actively rebelling or involved in violence against the regime.
  • Removed Insurgents (Gray): Insurgents who have been neutralized or removed by the regime.

The goal of this simulation is to visualize the dynamic interactions between these groups over time, and allow the user to adjust key parameters that influence how people move from one group to another.

Components of the Simulation

Canvas

What you see: Stick figures on the canvas, each represented by a color based on their group (Supporters, Dissidents, Insurgents, or Removed Insurgents). These stick figures emit random particles (letters) to give the visual impression of action.

Behavior: Over time, these stick figures will change color as they transition between groups based on the parameters controlled by the user through the interface.

Data Display on the Left

This section shows the real-time population of each group and provides feedback on how the population is evolving:

  • Supporters: The current number of people supporting the regime.
  • Dissidents: The current number of people opposing the regime.
  • Insurgents: The current number of people actively rebelling.
  • Removed Insurgents: The current number of insurgents neutralized by the regime.

Feedback includes:

  • Total Opposition: Sum of the Dissidents and Insurgents (i.e., total number of people opposing the regime).
  • Insurgent Fraction: The percentage of the total population that are insurgents.
  • Dissident Fraction: The percentage of the total population that are dissidents.

Interaction with the Simulation (dat.GUI Controls)

You can control how the simulation evolves using a panel on the right-hand side with sliders that adjust different variables. These variables are grouped into several categories:

Group Population Sliders

These sliders control the initial population of each group. Moving these sliders will instantly change the number of people in each group at the start of the simulation:

  • Supporters: Controls how many people start out as supporters (Green).
  • Dissidents: Controls how many people start out as dissidents (Orange).
  • Insurgents: Controls how many people start out as insurgents (Red).
  • Removed Insurgents: Controls how many people start out as removed insurgents (Gray).

Adjusting these sliders will reinitialize the stick figures on the canvas to reflect the new distribution.

Flow Rate Sliders

These sliders control how fast people transition between the groups based on various factors:

  • Recruitment Rate: The rate at which Dissidents are recruited into Insurgents. A higher value means more Dissidents will become Insurgents faster.
  • Becoming Dissident Rate: The rate at which Supporters become Dissidents. A higher value indicates a faster transition from Supporters to Dissidents.
  • Removing Insurgents Rate: The rate at which Insurgents are neutralized and become Removed Insurgents.

Parameters Influencing Flow Rates

These parameters affect the transition rates mentioned above:

  • Propensity to be Recruited: Affects the Recruitment Rate. A higher value means Dissidents are more likely to become Insurgents.
  • Regime Efforts: Affects the Removing Insurgents Rate. A higher value indicates the regime is more effective at neutralizing insurgents.
  • Protest Intensity: Affects the Becoming Dissident Rate. A higher value indicates more intense protests, leading to more Supporters becoming Dissidents.
  • Appeasement Rate: The rate at which Dissidents are turned back into Supporters by the regime’s appeasement efforts.

Messaging & Incident Factors

These factors influence how fast people move between groups based on external messaging and incidents:

  • Anti-Regime Message Intensity: Affects both the Becoming Dissident Rate and the Recruitment Rate. A higher value means more powerful anti-regime messages, causing more Supporters to become Dissidents and more Dissidents to become Insurgents.
  • Effect of Messages on Recruitment: Affects the Recruitment Rate. A higher value means anti-regime messages are more effective at recruiting Dissidents into Insurgents.
  • Frequency of Anti-Regime Messages: Affects both the Becoming Dissident Rate and the Recruitment Rate. A higher value means more frequent messages, leading to more transitions from Supporters to Dissidents and from Dissidents to Insurgents.
  • Violent Incident Intensity: Affects the Removing Insurgents Rate. A higher value means more intense violent incidents, allowing the regime to neutralize Insurgents faster.
  • Strength of Violent Incidents: Affects both the Becoming Dissident Rate and the Recruitment Rate. A higher value means violent incidents are more effective at increasing the number of Dissidents and Insurgents.

Other Variables

  • People Birth Rate: This variable adds new people to the Supporter group over time.
  • People Reaction Delay: This variable controls how long it takes for people to react to anti-regime messages and incidents. A higher value delays their reaction, slowing the transitions between groups.

How the Simulation Works

How Transitions Happen

The transitions between groups happen based on the flow rates you set using the sliders. For example:

  • If the Recruitment Rate is high, you will see more Dissidents (Orange) turn into Insurgents (Red) over time.
  • If the Removing Insurgents Rate is high, you will see more Insurgents (Red) turning into Removed Insurgents (Gray).
  • If the Protest Intensity is high, more Supporters (Green) will become Dissidents (Orange) faster.

Dynamic Feedback

As you adjust the sliders, the simulation updates in real-time:

  • Stickman Colors Change: Stick figures on the canvas will change colors to reflect the changes in group transitions.
  • Population Data Updates: The left-side panel will update to reflect the changes in population counts and percentages as people transition between groups.

Visual Elements

The stick figures represent the people, and their color changes as they move from one group to another. They also emit random particles (letters), adding a sense of action and dynamism to the simulation.

How to Use the Simulation

Follow these steps to use the simulation effectively:

  1. Set Initial Populations: Use the Supporters, Dissidents, Insurgents, and Removed Insurgents sliders to set the initial population of each group.
  2. Control Flow Rates: Use the Recruitment Rate, Becoming Dissident Rate, and Removing Insurgents Rate to control how fast people move between groups.
  3. Adjust Socio-Political Factors: Modify the Messaging, Incident Factors, and Other Variables (e.g., Propensity to be Recruited, Protest Intensity, Anti-Regime Message Intensity) to influence how messaging and violence impact the population dynamics.
  4. Observe Real-Time Changes: Watch the stickmen change colors and the data panel update in real time as you adjust the sliders.
  5. Experiment: Play with different settings to explore how different regimes of messaging, violence, and appeasement affect the dynamics between Supporters, Dissidents, Insurgents, and Removed Insurgents.
