Reading the simulation

How political states change

This experiment represents a small, hypothetical population. Each coloured 3D stick figure stands for one person. Green represents supporters, amber represents dissidents, red represents insurgents, and grey represents people who have left the active population through removal.

How to read the people and their messages

Each figure represents an individual within the population. Green represents support, amber dissent, red insurgency and grey removal. The figures stay in place so a change in colour is easy to follow.

Amber and red figures release visible words from their mouths, which drift forward and upward before fading. Red speech is brighter and more frequent. These marks are a visual metaphor for expression; they do not spread between people or change anyone's probability of switching state.

What makes the colours change?

At each time step, each person has a chance of changing state. Supporters may become dissidents; dissidents may return to support or become insurgents; and insurgents may be removed from the active population. Removed people stay in the grey state. The model does not permit other transitions.

pa→b = min(0.35, λa→b · ma→b)

The probability pa→b describes a transition from state a to state b during one step. The base rate is λa→b, while ma→b adjusts that rate using the conditions selected in the panel. Each transition probability is capped at 35% per step. The birth rate is capped at 12% per step, and available positions limit the number of new people. For dissidents, the return-to-support and recruitment probabilities are evaluated together, allowing no more than one transition in a step.

How to experiment

Use the original right-hand toolbar to adjust the population counts, flow rates, messaging, incidents and regime responses. Reset and compare different settings. The factors adjust probabilities rather than causing deterministic outcomes. Rotate the scene to look at people from different angles; click a figure to inspect its current state. Pause or advance one step to see a single round of transitions.

What this model cannot tell us

All eighteen controls retain their original labels. Population sliders reset the population; the remaining sliders modify bounded transition probabilities or the visual expression of speech. The rate factors are illustrative, not fitted to real political data. The figures are arranged for legibility, not according to observed geographic locations or social networks. Speech is an illustration of expression, not a mechanism of persuasion or observed messages. People change state according to fixed transition rates, not through contact with their neighbours. This is not a prediction of real political violence.

The 3D display requires WebGL and a connection to load Three.js; the original dat.GUI panel is loaded from the local project vendor library. Nothing is sent to a server; the population simulation runs in the browser.
