The model

How belonging becomes movement

This is a small agent-based experiment, not a prediction of real people. Each of the 120 figures has sixteen individual characteristics and can be drawn toward six kinds of community: family, friends, work, neighbourhood, nation, and religion or spirituality.

1. Community attachment

For each person, the model calculates an attachment score for every community using three relevant personal factors. Each factor blends the person's baseline (70%) with its shared slider setting (30%).

xik = 0.70 bik + 0.30 sk
Aig = (xig1 + xig2 + xig3) / 3

b is a person's baseline characteristic, s is the slider value, and A is attachment. All are scaled from 0 to 1. The group with the largest attachment score becomes the person's primary affiliation.

2. Belonging score

Attachment is combined with five social conditions. The score increases with acceptance, trust, economic security and diversity exposure; social friction lowers it.

Bi = clip(0.28Ai + 0.24a + 0.18t + 0.14e + 0.12d − 0.22f + 0.07 + εi + qi, 0, 1)

B is belonging, A the strongest attachment, a acceptance, t trust, e economic security, d diversity exposure and f friction. ε represents a fixed individual difference; q is a small time-varying fluctuation. clip keeps the result between 0 and 1.

3. From score to position

The model assigns an illustrative state: integrated at 0.70 or above, peripheral from 0.52, excluded from 0.30, and isolated below 0.30. A person can instead become a bridge when their two strongest attachments differ by less than 0.10, diversity exposure is at least 47, segregation pressure is below 85, and their belonging score is at least 0.42.

Integrated people stay near their community centre; peripheral, excluded and isolated people are placed progressively farther away. Bridges move between two communities. Segregation pressure spreads the six community centres apart. Each figure gradually travels toward its calculated destination.

4. Run an experiment

Open Controls and increase acceptance or trust, then watch the counts and positions change. Raise friction to test the opposite effect. Change diversity exposure to see when bridge positions emerge. Select a figure to inspect its score. Drag to orbit the camera and scroll to zoom.

What this model does not claim

The equations, weights, cutoffs and 120 people are hypothetical. They do not come from survey estimates, and spatial distance is only a visual metaphor for belonging. The experiment shows how assumptions produce patterns, not how any actual community behaves.
