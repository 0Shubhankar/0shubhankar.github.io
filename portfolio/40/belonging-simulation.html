This visualization represents a dynamic Belonging Simulation where each stickman figure represents an individual. These individuals are grouped based on six different types of social belonging: Family Connections, Friendship & Social Circles, Workplace Belonging, Local Community Involvement, National Identity, and Religious & Spiritual Community. Each group is visualized using a specific color:

  • Red: Family Connections
  • Blue: Friendship & Social Circles
  • Green: Workplace Belonging
  • Yellow: Local Community Involvement
  • Purple: National Identity
  • Orange: Religious & Spiritual Community

Each stickman can move between these groups depending on certain variables that define their level of attachment to each group, such as income, health, job satisfaction, and social connections. The simulation allows the user to observe how different factors impact an individual's sense of belonging and how they move between different social categories over time.

Key Features:

Dynamic Group Assignment: Each stickman belongs to one of the six groups based on a set of personal variables. The group they belong to is determined by their strongest sense of belonging, calculated from several factors.

Movement: Stickmen dynamically move between the six groups based on changes in their belonging scores. For example, if a stickman’s job satisfaction increases, they might move to the Workplace Belonging group, whereas lower income might cause them to move toward Family Connections.

Hover Interaction: When hovering over a stickman, details about the individual’s variables (such as income, job satisfaction, civic engagement) are displayed, allowing you to see the values driving their sense of belonging.

Real-time Updates: The simulation continuously updates in real-time. Stickmen adjust their scores slightly at every frame, which may cause them to switch groups based on their recalculated scores.

Group Information: On the bottom right, the number of stickmen in each group is displayed, providing real-time information on how many individuals belong to each category.

Setting and Control Panel (Using dat.GUI):

The right side of the screen contains a dat.GUI panel that allows you to adjust the range of several key variables that affect the belonging calculation. These include:

  • Closeness to Family: How close someone feels to their family.
  • Health: Overall physical and mental well-being.
  • Income: Economic standing and financial stability.
  • Social Connections: The strength of friendships and social ties.
  • Work-life Balance: How balanced one's professional and personal life is.
  • Openness to Diversity: Comfort with different cultures and ideas.
  • Job Satisfaction: Level of happiness with their work.
  • Civic Engagement: Involvement in local activities and communities.
  • National Pride: Sense of attachment to the nation.
  • Religious Participation: How much they participate in religious activities.

When you adjust a slider in the control panel, 20 randomly selected stickmen will immediately update based on the new setting, allowing you to explore how the changes in these variables influence their sense of belonging.

Context from the Report:

This simulation is based on a concept that explores how different socio-economic and personal factors influence an individual’s sense of belonging to different social groups. The idea is inspired by real-world dynamics where factors like income, health, and social connections drive our attachment to family, friends, work, community, and religion.

In this context, the simulation serves to visualize how fluid an individual's belonging can be, influenced by changes in life circumstances. It also demonstrates how different societal factors might affect population distributions across these social categories. For example, an increase in job satisfaction might lead to a greater sense of belonging in the workplace, while financial struggles might make individuals rely more on family or community support.

How the System Works:

Variables: The simulation tracks a set of variables for each stickman (individual), such as income, social connections, and job satisfaction. Each group has a unique set of variables that drive a stickman’s sense of belonging to that group.

Score Calculation: Every stickman has a belonging score for each group, based on a weighted combination of the relevant variables. For example, for the Family Connections group, the key variables include closeness to family, health, and income (normalized to avoid it dominating the score). The group with the highest score is where the stickman belongs.

Dynamic Movement: As each stickman’s variables change slightly over time, their belonging scores are recalculated. When a stickman’s highest score shifts to a different group, they visually move to that group’s designated area on the screen.

Conclusion:

This visualization provides an interactive way to explore how different life factors influence our social belonging. By tweaking the settings and observing how stickmen move between groups, you can gain insights into the fluid nature of social attachment and how various factors interplay in determining where people feel most connected.
