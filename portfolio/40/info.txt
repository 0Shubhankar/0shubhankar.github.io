To mathematically define how individuals (dots) move between the different communities (family, friends, workplace, local community, nation, religion), we need to establish a set of **factors** that affect **belonging scores**. Each dot will have a score for each community (family, friends, workplace, local community, nation, religion), and the community with the highest score will determine its group/color.

### Belonging Score Function:
For each individual \( i \), we can define a belonging score \( B_i \) for each community (family, friends, workplace, local community, nation, religion) based on influencing factors \( F_j \). The score for each community can be expressed as:

\[
B_{i, \text{community}} = \sum_{j} w_j F_j(i)
\]

Where:
- \( B_{i, \text{community}} \) is the belonging score for an individual \( i \) for a specific community.
- \( F_j(i) \) is the value of the influencing factor \( j \) for individual \( i \) (such as income, age, etc.).
- \( w_j \) is the weight assigned to factor \( F_j \), indicating its importance for that community's belonging score.

### Factors and Influence on Each Community:
Each community will have specific factors that heavily influence belonging. Here’s how we can define those factors mathematically for each community.

#### 1. **Family Belonging (Red)**
Key factors:
- **Closeness to Family** \( C_f \): A high value increases belonging to family.
- **Health** \( H \): Poor health increases reliance on family support, raising family belonging.
- **Income** \( I \): Lower income increases dependence on family.
  
Family belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{family}} = w_1 C_f + w_2 H + w_3 (1/I)
\]

#### 2. **Friends Belonging (Blue)**
Key factors:
- **Social Connections** \( S_c \): More social connections with friends increase belonging.
- **Work-life Balance** \( W_b \): Less work stress increases social time with friends.
- **Openness to Diversity** \( D_o \): A positive attitude toward diversity increases friendship connections.
  
Friends belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{friends}} = w_1 S_c + w_2 W_b + w_3 D_o
\]

#### 3. **Workplace Belonging (Green)**
Key factors:
- **Job Satisfaction** \( J_s \): Higher job satisfaction increases workplace belonging.
- **Income** \( I \): Higher income increases workplace attachment.
- **Workplace Inclusivity** \( W_i \): Inclusivity policies raise belonging.
  
Workplace belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{workplace}} = w_1 J_s + w_2 I + w_3 W_i
\]

#### 4. **Local Community Belonging (Yellow)**
Key factors:
- **Diversity in Local Community** \( D_l \): More diversity increases local belonging for those who are open to it.
- **Civic Engagement** \( C_e \): More involvement in local actions and events increases local community belonging.
- **Local Trust** \( T_l \): Trust in neighbors and local government increases belonging.
  
Local community belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{local}} = w_1 D_l + w_2 C_e + w_3 T_l
\]

#### 5. **National Belonging (Purple)**
Key factors:
- **National Pride** \( N_p \): A high sense of national pride increases belonging.
- **Political Stability** \( P_s \): Perception of political stability increases national belonging.
- **Income** \( I \): Higher income can increase attachment to national identity.
  
National belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{national}} = w_1 N_p + w_2 P_s + w_3 I
\]

#### 6. **Religious Belonging (Orange)**
Key factors:
- **Religious Participation** \( R_p \): Frequency of religious activity increases religious belonging.
- **Spiritual Needs** \( S_n \): The need for spiritual support or community increases religious belonging.
- **Cultural Support for Religion** \( C_r \): Societal support for religious practices influences belonging.
  
Religious belonging score:
\[
B_{i, \text{religion}} = w_1 R_p + w_2 S_n + w_3 C_r
\]

### Movement Between Groups:
For each dot \( i \), calculate all six belonging scores:

B_{i, \text{family}}, B_{i, \text{friends}}, B_{i, \text{workplace}}, B_{i, \text{local}}, B_{i, \text{national}}, B_{i, \text{religion}}

The group that has the highest score determines the current color of the dot. If a factor changes (e.g., increase in income, change in political stability, or religious participation), the belonging scores are recalculated, and the dot moves to the group with the new highest score.
