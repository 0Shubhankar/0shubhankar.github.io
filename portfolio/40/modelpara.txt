### 1. **Groups and Belonging Scores**

In the simulation, we have six groups representing different types of communities:
- **Family** (Red)
- **Friends** (Blue)
- **Workplace** (Green)
- **Local Community** (Yellow)
- **Nation** (Purple)
- **Religion** (Orange)

Each **dot** in the simulation represents a person, and that person has a **belonging score** for each group. The group with the highest score determines which group they belong to. For example, if someone’s highest score is for **Friends**, their dot will be colored **blue**.

### 2. **How to Calculate Belonging Scores**

For each person, the score for a group is influenced by several **factors**. These factors could be things like:
- **Closeness to family**
- **Job satisfaction**
- **Social connections**
- **Income**
- **Religious participation**
  
Each of these factors contributes to a person’s overall score for a particular group. To calculate the belonging score for each group, we use the following formula:

\[
B_{\text{group}} = w_1 \times F_1 + w_2 \times F_2 + w_3 \times F_3 + \dots
\]

Where:
- \( B_{\text{group}} \) is the belonging score for that group.
- \( F_1, F_2, F_3, \dots \) are the factors influencing that group.
- \( w_1, w_2, w_3, \dots \) are the weights assigned to each factor (how important that factor is for belonging to that group).

### 3. **Factors for Each Group**

Let’s define the key factors that influence the score for each group:

#### **Family (Red)**
- **Closeness to Family (C_f)**: How close they feel to their family.
- **Health (H)**: People with poor health may rely more on family.
- **Income (I)**: People with lower income may rely more on family for support.

Belonging score formula for Family:

\[
B_{\text{family}} = w_1 \times C_f + w_2 \times H + w_3 \times \left(\frac{1}{I}\right)
\]

#### **Friends (Blue)**
- **Social Connections (S_c)**: How many friends or social contacts they have.
- **Work-life Balance (W_b)**: People with less work stress may have more time for friends.
- **Openness to Diversity (D_o)**: Openness to diverse friendships increases belonging.

Belonging score formula for Friends:

\[
B_{\text{friends}} = w_1 \times S_c + w_2 \times W_b + w_3 \times D_o
\]

#### **Workplace (Green)**
- **Job Satisfaction (J_s)**: People with higher job satisfaction are more attached to their workplace.
- **Income (I)**: Higher income increases workplace attachment.
- **Workplace Inclusivity (W_i)**: Inclusive policies increase belonging.

Belonging score formula for Workplace:

\[
B_{\text{workplace}} = w_1 \times J_s + w_2 \times I + w_3 \times W_i
\]

#### **Local Community (Yellow)**
- **Diversity in Local Community (D_l)**: Living in a diverse area can influence local belonging.
- **Civic Engagement (C_e)**: Participation in local events increases belonging.
- **Local Trust (T_l)**: Trust in neighbors or local government increases local community belonging.

Belonging score formula for Local Community:

\[
B_{\text{local}} = w_1 \times D_l + w_2 \times C_e + w_3 \times T_l
\]

#### **Nation (Purple)**
- **National Pride (N_p)**: High national pride increases belonging to the nation.
- **Political Stability (P_s)**: People feel more connected to a stable nation.
- **Income (I)**: Higher income can increase national belonging.

Belonging score formula for Nation:

\[
B_{\text{national}} = w_1 \times N_p + w_2 \times P_s + w_3 \times I
\]

#### **Religion (Orange)**
- **Religious Participation (R_p)**: Regular participation in religious activities increases religious belonging.
- **Spiritual Needs (S_n)**: People seeking spiritual fulfillment might be more attached to religious communities.
- **Cultural Support for Religion (C_r)**: Society’s support for religious practices influences belonging.

Belonging score formula for Religion:

\[
B_{\text{religion}} = w_1 \times R_p + w_2 \times S_n + w_3 \times C_r
\]

### 4. **Assigning a Group**

Once you calculate the belonging scores for each group, you compare them to find the highest score. The group with the highest score becomes that person's current group.

For example:
- **Family score**: 0.505
- **Friends score**: 0.66
- **Workplace score**: 0.52
- **Local Community score**: 0.64
- **Nation score**: 0.48
- **Religion score**: 0.39

Since the **Friends** score is the highest, the person would belong to the **Friends** group and be colored **blue**.

### 5. **Simulating Changes in Belonging**

You can simulate changes in people’s lives that affect their belonging to different groups. For example, if their **job satisfaction** improves or their **religious participation** increases, their scores for **Workplace** or **Religion** might increase, causing them to switch to those groups.

Let’s say a person’s **job satisfaction** improves from 0.8 to 1.0, and their **religious participation** increases from 0.3 to 0.7. We update those values and recalculate their scores for **Workplace** and **Religion**:

- **Updated Workplace score**: 0.62
- **Updated Religion score**: 0.59

Now, **Friends** (0.66) is still the highest, but **Workplace** and **Religion** are becoming more competitive. If more changes occur, like an increase in workplace inclusivity or a decrease in national pride, the person could move to **Workplace** or **Religion**.

### 6. **Real-Time Changes**

In the simulation, these factors can change dynamically over time based on:
- **Societal trends** (e.g., increase in secularization, political instability)
- **Personal life events** (e.g., job loss, new friendships, joining a religious community)

When factors change, people (dots) will shift between groups in real-time, making the simulation fluid and engaging.

### 7. **How It Looks**

On the visual side, each group will be represented by a color. The dots (people) will move between different colored groups depending on their highest score. If a person's highest belonging is with their friends, they’ll be **blue**; if it's with their religion, they’ll be **orange**.
