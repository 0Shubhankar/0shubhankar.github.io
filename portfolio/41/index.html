WHO DOES THE CITY OWE?
New capital bonds are assumed to be 10-year bullet bonds. Their coupon depends on a simple fiscal-risk spread at issuance; it is fixed thereafter. Legacy principal amortizes $1M yearly. Emergency notes are interest-only in this four-year game. Debt-service coverage uses operating revenue minus service costs divided by annual interest and scheduled principal.

All figures are fictional and deterministic; no official credit rating, yield quote, public accounting standard or financing recommendation is implied. Transfers and fees are simplified. This model excludes restricted funds, capital asset accounting, pensions, bond covenants, debt issuance approvals and variable macroeconomic conditions.

THE EXPERIMENT

It's not a spreadsheet.
It's a city with consequences.

The Treasury Edition adds annual tax and service policy, an operating budget, debt-service coverage and simulated borrowing costs to the four-year story. Bonds raise cash now but create future interest payments and issuance costs. Development can expand the tax base—but infrastructure costs money. Disasters force difficult choices. At the end, you don't get one score; you see the city you left behind.

Everything here is fictional. The city, events, costs, public reactions and simplified financial rules are illustrative game mechanics, not forecasts, real credit ratings, investment advice, or a complete municipal budget. The accounting for borrowing, capital spending and debt service is intentionally explicit so future versions can add more realism without losing the fun.

BEFORE YOU TAKE OFFICE

You can't fix everything.

Make one story decision and choose annual tax and service policies each year. Watch cash, debt, public approval, city services and the miniature city respond. Each year, routine income pays for operating services and scheduled debt payments; projects, borrowing and emergencies affect the rest.

Choose carefully. The game is designed to make you trade off short-term popularity, financial resilience and long-term public benefits.

Tip: You can click on buildings, the bridge and the park to inspect the city. You can replay the term as many times as you like.
