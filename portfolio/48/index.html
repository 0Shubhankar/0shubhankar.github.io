HOW THE NUMBERS WORK.

No real-world project is represented. All amounts and scores are hypothetical. The model separates one-off land-value capture from recurring taxes, public benefits from agency cash flows, and a bond's principal from its interest.

EXPAND THE FINANCIAL AND CITY FORMULAS +

Ridership. For each selected stop, the model estimates district catchment using walking distance, local population and jobs. Adjacent stations overlap; projected riders increase with access and city jobs but decrease when fares rise. Scenarios modify demand, with a staged ridership ramp.

Construction and funding. Capital cost combines station cost, route length and elevated/underground factors. Grant funding is a percentage of project cost. Land-value capture is a one-off contribution based on a chosen percentage of estimated land uplift, with a collection cap. Bond principal is the elected share of remaining capital cost; the unfinanced portion is a city capital contribution.

Debt and operations. Bonds have level annual payments over 30 years; the coupon equals the assumed base rate plus scenario spread. Each year, agency operating cash is fare revenue plus dedicated annual tax uplift minus operating costs. Debt service is principal plus interest. The annual municipal subsidy is the shortfall between agency operating cash and debt service. Negative cash isn't hidden: it shows as a fiscal funding gap.

Economic value. Reported social benefit/cost is an illustrative 30-year present-value comparison of travel-time and external benefits against construction and operations. Property uplift is tracked separately to avoid counting the same accessibility benefit twice.

Equity. New walking access is calculated separately for lower-income districts. A modeled displacement-pressure score responds to speculative land uplift and the affordable-housing agreement; it is an indicator, not an observed relocation count.

Uncertainty. Scenario outcomes are deterministic stress assumptions, not probability forecasts or real municipal credit ratings. See README for variable definitions and known limitations.
